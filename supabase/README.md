# Backend contract

`202611120001_bulk_stock.sql` makes cylinder and bulk KG consume FIFO stock together and makes physical credit notes restore both. It rebuilds existing owners' derived caches; source documents, billing fields, collections and cylinder counts are unchanged. Apply it before syncing clients that use the bulk-stock rule.

`202611130002_share_purchase.sql` whitelists `sharePurchase` (a live purchase's reference) on sales and credit notes and validates it. `rebuild` first reserves, in document order, each such share's KG from that purchase (so they never enter FIFO and the purchase stocks in the rest at the rest of its bill), allocating it when both are replayed; a share returned by a physical credit note reserves nothing. Changed shares must match `round_div(extraGrams*rate, rateUnit*100)*100` of their purchase, or their FIFO cost rounded to whole rupees; a changed purchase must not leave its shares short, and a purchase whose reference no longer exists may not be voided while unreturned shares name it.

`202611130001_fifo_backorders.sql` replaces the provisional `cogs:shortage` posting with costed backorders (above), stops `rebuild` from raising for a stored share that lost its stock (it waits like any sale; only shares changed by the command are verified, after the whole replay, so a share may be priced from a later purchase), and makes a financial-only credit note of a share post the shared FIFO cost to `cogs`. It rebuilds existing owners' caches under their sync locks without touching documents or the sequence.

`202611120002_purchase_sharing.sql` adds `bulkAtCost` and exact-paisa `bulkCost` to sale/credit-note canonical fields. Shared bulk transfers FIFO inventory cost to `purchase-share:cost`, with the recorded party charge offsetting that recovery account; it contributes neither sales revenue nor COGS. Mixed documents consume and cost cylinders first. FIFO's `cost_account` column lets physical credit notes reverse each cost category. The changed-document ids from `apply_command` are passed in a transaction-local setting so newly posted shares must match actual FIFO cost and available, known-cost stock. Rebuilds after later purchase corrections preserve the recorded share price and expose a recovery difference. Applying the migration rebuilds existing owners' caches under sync locks, without rewriting documents or sync sequence. Deploy it before syncing clients that write these fields; previous canonicalisers would drop them.

All mutation is through `apply_command(p_command jsonb)`. The authenticated user is the owner; owner IDs are never accepted from the payload. An account is one organization's books: the app can hold several organizations on a device, each signed into its own account, and refuses to link two of them to the same one. The server needs nothing for this beyond the per-owner separation it already has. Business records, postings, FIFO lots, command receipts and changes have owner-read RLS and no direct client write grants.

A command has `command_id`, `device_id`, `epoch`, `action`, `reason`, and `changes`. Each change has `id`, `kind`, `data`, `expected_version`. The action is save/correct/void/restore/close/reopen. New records expect version 0. Posted document content cannot be edited: correction atomically voids the old record and creates a replacement.

Idempotency hashes the canonical JSONB payload. The same UUID with the same payload returns the existing receipt. Reusing its UUID with changed content is rejected. A nested transaction savepoint rolls back every financial and record write if validation fails, while retaining a structured rejection receipt.

The per-owner sync row is locked until commit. Its ordinary bigint counter is advanced in the transaction, producing a commit-ordered change feed without timestamp or sequence-allocation races. Clients apply complete bundles and the cursor in one SQLite transaction; they never advance the cursor to an apply response and skip other-device changes.

`sync_status()` bootstraps cash/business defaults only and returns epoch, sequence, protocol. `pull_changes(epoch, after, limit)` returns up to 100 complete bundles. An uncertain send is resolved by sending the same command again: `apply_command` answers it from its stored receipt (there is no separate lookup RPC; `202611100003_drop_lookup_command.sql` removed it). Feed bundles also contain server-generated audit records for cross-device history. V1 retains the feed without compaction.

The server rebuilds deterministic financial/stock caches after financial commands, sorted by business date, effective timestamp, then document UUID. Cache rebuilding is intentionally simple for a single business. Source documents and the complete immutable change feed preserve provenance. For larger deployments, replace full replay with checkpoints after the earliest changed date while retaining the same RPC contract and tests.

Gas taken with no stock waits as a FIFO row without a lot; the next stock-in (`khata_private.stock_in`) covers waiting rows first in `ordinal` order at its own cost, posting under the waiting document dated the stock-in day (`202611130001_fifo_backorders.sql`). A physical credit note cancels its invoice's waiting rows. Backdated cost corrections replay FIFO. Closing locks all dates through the selected day; reopening invalidates later closes. Stock and deposit over-returns are rejected, while negative business gas/full-cylinder stock is visible for reconciliation.

`reset_dataset(epoch)` (Settings → Reset all data, `202611100001_reset_dataset.sql`) is the owner's full erase. Given the current epoch, it deletes every row the owner has in every `khata_*` table that has an `owner_id` column, so new tables are covered automatically. It keeps the owner's `khata_sync` row, sets a fresh epoch and sequence 0, and returns the new status. Old-epoch pulls and commands then fail, and the next `sync_status()` bootstraps the defaults. Any new per-owner table must use the `khata_` prefix and an `owner_id` column, or reset will not erase it.

Cloud backup restore is an administrative maintenance operation. Rotate the owner's epoch before devices reconnect. Ordinary app backup restore never replaces cloud data.

Documents are canonicalised per entry type before storage. `form` (the Roznamcha form an entry was made in) is kept for every type and has no accounting effect; `202611070002_roznamcha_form.sql` adds it and the parchi (`vehicle`) on receipts.

`202611110001_direct_to_party.sql` adds Direct to Party: `direct` (paisa a customer paid straight to another party) and `directParty` on sales and receipts. `rebuild` posts `ap:<directParty>` debit / `ar:<party>` credit, with no money account. `validate_document` allows it only on a sale or a customer's receipt, to an existing, not deleted party other than the customer, and counts it as collection; `apply_command` treats a party that was paid directly as having entries. It must be applied before clients use the fields, or they are dropped on canonicalisation.

`202611110002_bank_lines.sql` adds optional `directBank` text and ordered `bankLines` on sales and receipts. Each line has integer paisa `amount` and either an `account` or a direct `party` with optional `bankName`; validation limits the array to 20 lines and bank names to 120 characters, rejects cash accounts and missing/deleted/self direct parties, and includes all amounts in collection/allocation checks. Rebuild preserves legacy bank/direct postings and then posts each additional line; party deletion checks also cover line-only references. Bank names have no accounting effect. Apply this migration before cloud-linked clients use these optional fields, or canonicalisation drops them. Old device documents need no migration. `tool/backend_test.dart` includes client/server posting parity, canonicalisation, invalid-line and deletion checks.
